The problem

Batteries are easy to buy and hard to run well.

An operator has to plan around grid outages, prices that change by the hour and battery wear, every day. Asking an AI to write the plan sounds easy, but a plan written as text can quietly break physics: charge the battery past full, or count on energy that is not there when the grid goes down.

How it works

Four steps. Only one of them is AI.

  1. Step 1

    You say the goal

    In your own words and language: an outage, a reserve to keep, a price to dodge.

  2. Step 2

    AI turns it into rules

    Muse Spark Open writes exact, checkable rules, and a separate AI review checks they match what you asked.

  3. Step 3

    An optimizer plans the day

    A mathematical optimizer finds the cheapest hour-by-hour battery plan that meets every rule.

  4. Step 4

    A checker verifies, then replays it on real weather

    Physics checks the plan before anything runs; then it is replayed on the same-day weather next to a simple rule-based battery.

The AI proposes. Deterministic tools decide.

    Honest limits

    What this demo is, and is not.

    • Synthetic sites. Sites, tariffs, prices and loads are illustrative, not real customer data. No real device is controlled.
    • Modelled weather. The "same-day weather" in replays is a weather model's estimate of what happened, not a measurement.
    • Whole site kept running. During an outage the optimizer keeps every load on, so the rule-based battery can look cheaper by switching other loads off. The results say so when it happens.
    • Limited live runs. Live AI runs are rate-limited; when they are not available the page plays a recorded run and labels it.
    • A small world. Results cover only the sites in this demo: a cold store in India, a clinic in Kenya and a household in Germany.
